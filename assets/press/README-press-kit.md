## Short synopsis

*Notes on Care, Risk & Knowledge* brings together research, fragments, poems, and images produced in Brazil. Moving across public health, institutions, memory, and everyday life, the book asks how care is reorganized when protocols and certainty no longer suffice.

## Long synopsis

Written, photographed, and assembled in Brazil, *Notes on Care, Risk & Knowledge* is a hybrid academic and visual book about public health, institutions, crisis, memory, and the fragile forms of care through which collective life is sustained. Research fragments, short essays, poems, and images move across the history of health, documentary practices, institutional life, and the production of knowledge under pressure. Through cases involving Zika, yellow fever, and a suspected Ebola case, the book treats Brazil not as a peripheral example, but as an analytical ground from which broader questions about risk, care, inequality, expertise, and public institutions can be reconsidered.

## Author bio — 100 words

Maria Carolina Fóscolo Gomes is a Brazilian historian, researcher, author, and publisher based in Belo Horizonte. At the Federal University of Minas Gerais, she works at the intersection of the history of health, organizational studies, archives, and oral history, examining how institutions learn, improvise, remember, and sustain care under pressure. She is the author of *Notes on Care, Risk & Knowledge* and founder of Fóscolo & Company, an independent publishing project created to bring intellectual rigor, visual form, and literary sensibility into sustained conversation. Her research has circulated through publications and international academic events.
