There is no single way into this book.

An essay on research, fragments, poems, and images—and what changes when a work refuses to offer only one point of entry.

Editorial essay · 5-minute read

By Maria Carolina Fóscolo Gomes

Some books begin with a thesis. Others begin with a story. Notes on Care, Risk & Knowledge begins with a question: what do we do when familiar forms of explanation can no longer contain experience?

Historical research offers archives, interviews, protocols, and institutional traces. It makes it possible to reconstruct decisions, understand conflicts, and observe knowledge moving under pressure. Yet not everything a crisis produces belongs to the language of proof. There are silences, hesitations, and gestures of care whose importance does not diminish because they resist reduction to a category.

A fragment is not what remains of an unfinished text. It is a way of preserving what would lose its force if compelled to appear whole.

The book therefore places different registers in proximity. The essay sustains the question; the poem changes the rhythm; photography restores thickness to space; the archive resists seamless memory. None illustrates the other. They remain beside one another, producing a reading that is not linear, but attentive.

To enter through yellow fever is to observe disputed protocols and expertise. To enter through Zika is to see risk traverse bodies, families, and public responsibility. To enter through a suspected Ebola case is to follow the practical intelligence that emerges when prepared answers no longer suffice.

There is no single way into this book because there is no single way of knowing. Reading may begin with an argument, a pause, or a detail. Care is not merely the application of a correct answer. Often, it is learning to pay attention before an answer exists.
